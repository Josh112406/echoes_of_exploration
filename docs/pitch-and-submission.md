# Pitch and Submission Plan

## Positioning

Lead with **Best Use of Storytelling**. Support the story with visible scientific validity, NASA data use, and deliberate art/technology integration.

## Thirty-second pitch

> NASA’s explorers do not stop mattering when the headlines move on. *Echoes of Exploration* turns nine pieces of Moon, Mars, asteroid, and deep-space hardware into short playable memories. Players do what the machines did—land, measure, sample, fly, map, and communicate—then inspect the real evidence and NASA sources behind each interaction. The result is a private, accessible browser game and expandable mission archive that connects engineering choices to scientific discovery.

## Three-minute pitch structure

### 0:00–0:30 — Problem

Mission history is often presented as dense pages or isolated facts. School-age enthusiasts know names but may not see how instruments produced knowledge.

### 0:30–1:05 — Product

Show the quiet observatory, 3 × 3 campaign, broad catalog, and Signal Archivist premise. Explain that memory echoes are dramatized while evidence is sourced.

### 1:05–2:00 — Live journey

Demonstrate one short interaction—prefer Apollo 11 reflector or Viking 1—then open Behind the Memory:

- real hardware image;
- what the instrument measured;
- what the interaction simplified;
- conclusion and uncertainty;
- NASA source.

Quickly show Moon/Mars/Elsewhere breadth and another mechanic family.

### 2:00–2:30 — Trust and access

Show keyboard/touch, reduced motion, non-canvas alternative, local-only save, reset, privacy, attribution, and adjustable manifest.

### 2:30–3:00 — Impact and future

Explain that the same content system can add major missions without changing the game engine. End on the restored observatory and the line: **“A mission ends. Its evidence keeps traveling.”**

## Judge evidence map

| Theme | Evidence to show |
|---|---|
| Storytelling | Memory Echo → interaction → evidence → restored connection |
| Science | Instrument/action/evidence/conclusion/uncertainty chain |
| Data | NASA/PDS/challenge sources and provenance ledger |
| Art & technology | Authentic media paired with labeled illustration and data-driven Phaser interactions |
| Relevance | Moon, Mars, and elsewhere hardware; mixed functionality/status |
| Feasibility | Static deployment, three mechanic families, adjustable roster |
| Responsibility | WCAG target, no telemetry/accounts, rights and AI disclosure |

## Submission content checklist

- Project title and one-sentence summary.
- Challenge name and direct response.
- Problem, audience, and solution.
- Deployed Vercel or Netlify URL.
- Public source repository and open licenses.
- Nine-encounter roster and broader catalog description.
- NASA/challenge datasets and resources used.
- Scientific methodology and source links.
- Technology and architecture summary.
- Accessibility, privacy, and rights summary.
- Team members and numbered roles.
- AI-assistance disclosure and human validation process.
- Screenshots and demo link if requested.
- Known limitations: no learner evaluation, English-only release, no offline guarantee.
- Future roadmap without presenting it as completed.

## NASA data-use paragraph

> Mission content is curated from NASA Science and JPL mission records, the Planetary Data System and related NASA archives, challenge-provided resources, and item-level NASA media records. Data is normalized into version-controlled static content so the educational experience remains stable and source-linked. Each featured encounter explains the instrument or subsystem, the observation it produced, the conclusion supported by that evidence, and how gameplay simplified the real operation.

## Demo safety plan

- Use a clean browser profile with a pretested local save backup.
- Keep a direct link to the selected encounter.
- Have screenshots/video as fallback, but do not pretend recorded playback is live.
- Test venue network; static content does not call NASA APIs at runtime.
- Keep both Vercel and Netlify deployment options ready.

## Future roadmap

Only after the hackathon:

- More NASA and partner-agency missions.
- Teacher guide and optional lesson plans.
- Additional languages through prepared localization files.
- Narration with captions and rights-cleared voices.
- Formal learner/usability study before making effectiveness claims.
- Offline packaging if user research supports it.

## Final truth check

Do not say “complete inventory,” “NASA-approved,” “scientifically proven learning,” “real transmissions from dormant spacecraft,” or “all imagery is public domain.” Use the precise claims established in this package.
