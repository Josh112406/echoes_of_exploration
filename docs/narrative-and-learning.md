# Narrative and Learning Design

## Narrative premise

The Observatory of Echoes is complete physically but quiet. Its records have not been connected into stories. The player trains as a Signal Archivist, reconstructing nine memory echoes from mission records so visitors can understand not only *what* a machine was, but *how* it turned engineering into evidence.

The campaign has no villain or invented emergency. Its stakes are stewardship, understanding, and connection.

## Story structure

### Opening — “The Quiet Observatory”

The player selects or skips a local-only call sign. The Curator explains three rules:

1. Memory echoes are dramatizations reconstructed from records.
2. Evidence panels contain sourced facts.
3. A good Archivist separates what happened, what scientists inferred, and what the story imagines.

### Chapter I — Moon: Learning to Reach

Theme: before distant science, machines had to land, measure, and move safely.

### Chapter II — Mars: Reading a Changing World

Theme: instruments turn rocks, soil, air, motion, and unexpected events into clues.

### Chapter III — Elsewhere: Beyond the Familiar

Theme: distance changes everything—power, communication, timing, and what can be known.

### Ending — “The Archive Is Alive When It Is Used”

The final display connects all nine missions. The player is named Signal Archivist and invited into Free Explore. The ending avoids claiming that the artifacts themselves awakened.

## Voice system

Each encounter uses three labeled registers:

- **Memory Echo:** brief first-person, emotionally written dramatization.
- **Archivist Note:** plain-language sourced explanation.
- **Deep Dive:** instruments, measurements, uncertainty, and related mission data.

Example pattern, not final script:

> **Memory Echo:** “The surface rushed closer, then my legs felt the Moon.”
>
> **Archivist Note:** Surveyor 1 used an automated descent system; the player is interpreting simplified telemetry, not manually piloting it.

Never fabricate quotations from astronauts, engineers, or mission audio.

## Content layers

### Story

- 40–80 words per beat.
- One idea per paragraph.
- Defines unfamiliar terms in context.
- Suitable for the main age band without talking down to older players.

### Mission Console

- Hardware type and destination.
- Objective and operational constraint.
- Instrument or subsystem used.
- Evidence obtained and discovery enabled.
- Actual mission/hardware status.

### Deep Dive

- Instrument measurement and units where useful.
- How raw observations became interpretation.
- Uncertainty or competing explanation.
- Mission connection and primary sources.
- “How the game simplified this” note.

## Learning pattern

Every encounter follows:

1. **Question:** What must be discovered or accomplished?
2. **Constraint:** What makes it difficult?
3. **Action:** What does the hardware do?
4. **Observation:** What measurement or result appears?
5. **Inference:** Which conclusion is supported?
6. **Connection:** What later mission or present-day question did it influence?

## Concept coverage

| Concept | Encounters |
|---|---|
| Motion, forces, landing | Surveyor 1, NEAR Shoemaker |
| Light and measurement | Apollo 11 reflector |
| Energy, range, systems engineering | Apollo 17 LRV, Ingenuity, Voyager 1 |
| Geology and evidence | Apollo 17 LRV, Viking 1, Spirit, NEAR Shoemaker |
| Atmospheres | Viking 1, Ingenuity |
| Communication, storage, signal | Voyager 1, New Horizons |
| Scientific uncertainty | Viking 1, Spirit, Voyager 1 |

This mapping informs design; curriculum codes are not displayed to players.

## Tone guide

- Quiet mystery and rediscovery, ending in hope.
- Warm and curious, never mournful for its own sake.
- Humor comes from perspective and contrast, not mission failure.
- Failed and ended missions are treated as knowledge-building, not embarrassment.
- Avoid conquest language and unsupported claims that hardware is “abandoned trash.”

## Educational claim

The submission calls the project an **educational game**. It does not say that it improves scores, retention, interest, or understanding because no learner evaluation is planned. Internal QA verifies content and usability, not learning effectiveness.
